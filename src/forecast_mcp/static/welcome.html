Plan the right number of staff for every entrance

This tool predicts how many guests will arrive, shows how sure it is, and suggests staffing plans. You make the final choice.

The short walkthrough takes about a minute.

The problem

Every event has the same question: how many people should work at each entrance, and when?

Too few staff

Long queues, unhappy guests, people missing the show.

Too many staff

People standing around, and money spent that could go elsewhere.

Nobody knows exactly how many guests will come. Rain, a concert in town or a new car park can all change it.

What this tool does

  • Predicts arrivals
    For each entrance and hour: the most likely number of guests, plus a range for good and bad days.
  • Listens to what you know
    Tell it about roadworks, another event or a schedule change. Nothing changes until you confirm.
  • Suggests staffing plans
    From cheapest to shortest queues. You pick the balance that suits you.

You can use it on this website, or just by chatting with Claude.

About this prototype

This is a research prototype. Here is what is made up and what is calculated.

The park is made up

Parkland Theme Park, its three entrances and 120 days of visitor history are invented, built to behave like real arrivals: busy mornings, an evening rush before the show, rainy days, a brand-new gate.

The numbers are calculated

Nothing on screen is faked. Every forecast and plan is worked out live from that history by a forecasting model and an optimization algorithm.

The forecast is checked

On past days it hadn't seen, the forecast's likely ranges were checked against what really happened.

The methods are simple stand-ins

The model and the algorithm are deliberately simple, made to test the ideas. A real venue would plug in its own data and stronger models.

What we are testing: whether showing why a forecast is unsure, and turning your knowledge into rules the planner follows, helps you make better staffing decisions.
